Understanding Well-being

A country's progress isn't just about its economy. The OECD Better Life Index measures well-being across 11 crucial dimensions – from health and housing to work-life balance and community connections.

This tool brings that rich data to life, allowing you to see the world through a lens that values what truly matters for people's lives.

Key Stories From the Data

Nordic Nations: Consistently Top Performers

Year after year (2006-2023), countries like Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland, and Iceland consistently rank among the very best across nearly all aspects of well-being measured – from health to education to life satisfaction. It shows their success isn't accidental, but built on strong foundations.

Even their lowest-ranking dimension is still high compared to many other countries, showcasing remarkable across-the-board strength.

“Nordics aren’t just wealthy; they show a remarkable balance, excelling in health, trust, and work-life harmony all at once.”

Rising Stars: Lithuania & Korea's Journey

Some countries have made remarkable progress in improving well-being over the last decade or so. Lithuania and Korea stand out, showing impressive gains and demonstrating that positive change is achievable.

Significant strides in Income, Skills, and Safety improvements.
These nations showcase how focused efforts can lead to substantial improvements in quality of life across multiple important dimensions.

“See how focused efforts helped Lithuania and Korea significantly boost quality of life.”

The Pandemic's Shadow on Well-being

The COVID-19 pandemic caused a sharp drop in reported life satisfaction across many countries in 2020. While things have improved since the initial shock, the recovery has been slow and incomplete for many.

Sharp Dip (2020) → Partial Recovery → Plateau
A majority of countries still report lower well-being scores in 2023 compared to before the pandemic began in 2019.

“Economies bounced back faster than spirits. Our collective mood is still healing.”

Good for the Planet, Good for the Soul?

The data suggests a clear positive link: countries improving their environmental quality (like cleaner air) also tend to see a rise in their population's subjective well-being over time.

+
Estonia 🇪🇪 is a great example, showing significant improvements in both environmental quality and life satisfaction between 2006 and 2023.

“Investing in a cleaner environment might just be an investment in happiness too.”
